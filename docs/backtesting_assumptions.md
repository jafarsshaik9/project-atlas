# Phase 2B replay, costing and risk assumptions

**SYNTHETIC RESEARCH ONLY — NOT LIVE TRADING PERFORMANCE**

## Scope and chronology

The runner processes one instrument/session per run. LSE, NYSE and NASDAQ
fixtures share the same contracts, strategy, costing, accounting and risk code.
Multi-instrument accounting/reservations are supported and tested, but a combined
multi-instrument event runner and full walk-forward framework are future work.
The calendar and replay clock are injectable for future chronological folds.

Observation order is received/available UTC time, never bar-start time. The
contracts require event <= availability <= received. At a shared availability
timestamp, eligible stop-breaching quotes for an existing position take priority;
other ties retain bars-before-quotes and original observation order. This uses
only observations already available at that timestamp. An entry quote must have
an event time strictly after signal creation to fill. Publishing or receiving a historical bar later does not make its earlier
open price executable. Incomplete candles, gaps in required slots, duplicates,
quality flags, stale observations, mismatched units and future evidence prevent
new risk. A completed five-minute candle may be used only after receipt.

ORB v1 uses an explicitly configured 15-minute opening range on five-minute
candles by default. Every expected range/confirmation slot is required. The first
completed close strictly above the range high confirms a long hypothesis.
Stop: range midpoint rounded down to the instrument tick. Target: entry
hypothesis plus configurable 2R, rounded down. Minimum bar volume: artificial
100 units. Signal expires after five minutes or before the flat-by time.
Time exit is five minutes before that session's close, including half-days.
There is at most one identified hypothesis per instrument/session. Old triggers
are not rediscovered; failed/rejected hypotheses are not automatically retried.

No breakout is an order or evidence of positive expected net profitability.
RiskDecision.approved means only that this exact local simulation proposal
passes deterministic software policies. RankedOpportunity remains research-only;
no calibrated economic ranker, statistical edge or live trading approval exists.

## Local fills

Entry uses the first eligible next quote's ask, after independent risk validation.
Quantity is floored to the instrument step, constrained by cash, planned risk and
allocation, then rechecked atomically with reservation. Available ask size can
produce no fill or one partial fill; unfilled quantity is cancelled. No liquidity
is invented and unconfirmed sale proceeds are never spendable.

An opening fill is marked immediately at the known bid for equity/drawdown.
The fill candle's earlier high/low may precede entry, so it is excluded from
OHLC stop/target evaluation. A fresh, complete, correctly identified/unit-matched
quote observed strictly after entry can independently trigger the stop, including
inside that candle. It exits at its available bid with executable-price costs,
requiring full-position bid liquidity. A bid below the stop fills below the stop;
planned or stressed risk is not a loss cap.

An observed stop remains triggered when liquidity is insufficient. Subsequent
bars cannot turn it into a target win or invent a fill. The runner waits for a
fresh eligible quote with adequate bid size, conservatively using the worse of
its bid and the planned stop (no favorable recovery improvement). Without one,
the position remains open and is flagged. Pre-entry observations received late,
stale/incomplete/quality-flagged quotes and mismatched units cannot supply exits.
Later completed OHLC candles, before a quote-triggered stop, model standing exits:

- Stop hit: use the worse of stop and candle open; charge explicit synthetic
  spread/slippage/exit fees. A gap may lose far more than planned or stressed risk.
- Target hit without stop: use target, with no favourable gap improvement.
- Both hit: stop first, irrespective of the candle's closing direction.
- Time exit: use a fresh quote's bid at/after flat-by, with adequate bid size.
  A missing/stale/illiquid quote leaves the position open and prominently flagged.
  The report does not fabricate liquidation or a final cash balance.

Contingent bar fills are booked when the candle is received; exact intrabar
ordering is unknown. Bar exit liquidity/queueing is not modelled. Stop/time-exit
quotes require full-size liquidity; partial exits are supported by the ledger
but are not generated by this minimal quote exit model. These limitations prevent
interpreting the results as executable real-market performance.

## Synthetic costs and FX

examples/costs.synthetic.json is an explicitly artificial, effective-dated
schedule. It is not a Trading 212 fee or tax schedule. All components must be
known, including explicit zeros: spread, slippage, fixed/variable commission,
FX conversion, entry/exit transaction taxes and other charges. A missing,
ambiguous, out-of-date or inapplicable schedule prevents new risk.

Rates and money use Decimal; JSON monetary/rate values must be decimal strings.
Expenses round **up** per component to pennies. Cost stress multiplies explicit
costs by the configured synthetic factor. Actual fee and tax applicability,
rates, rounding conventions and FX spreads require independent future validation.

GBX divides by 100 before any GBP arithmetic. USD notionals convert to GBP at
a validated synthetic rate available at the decision/fill. Foreign cash is
instantaneously converted: the ledger tracks GBP cash and zero USD cash, with
conversion charges separately attributed in cost components. Retaining native
USD balances, settlement delays and multi-day FX revaluation are outside scope.
Fill history retains the charged component breakdown, and the report attributes
FX conversion expenses separately within total costs. Converted notionals and
carrying values retain Decimal precision internally; currency settlement rounding
is not assumed to reproduce any broker. Console amounts display two decimals.

Executable bid/ask prices already embed observed spread; their spread component
is zero to prevent double counting. Mid/bar-priced exits add half the configured
synthetic spread. Reported gross P&L uses actual simulated fill prices, so spread
embedded in those prices is not separately subtracted again. Reported costs are
the explicit cash expenses, with each side charged once. Fixed charges apply per
actual simulated fill. If additional partial fills would exceed the reserved
fee budget, they require a new risk check rather than overspending.

## Ledger and policies

PortfolioLedger is an in-memory, average-GBP-carrying-cost ledger. Entry expenses
enter basis; exit proceeds less expenses and allocated basis produce realized
P&L. Remaining positions hold proportional basis after partial exits.
Reconciliation checks nonnegative cash/reservations and the identity
cash + remaining cost basis = initial capital + realized P&L.
Equity uses current marks; both cumulative and daily P&L are available.
Marks and ledger operation timestamps must not travel backwards.

Reservations have versions and unique intent IDs. Approve-and-reserve holds a
single lock across independent risk recomputation and reservation, preventing
concurrent overspending. Fill IDs are unique. The complete original planned/
stressed risk transfers to the first partial entry fill, conservatively retained
when its remainder is cancelled. The first exit, including a partial exit,
cancels any outstanding entry remainder before risk can be released. That
remainder cannot reopen the position; its intent ID remains consumed.
Open positions retain the full approved planned/stressed risk envelope through
partial exits until flat. This is a conservative allocation, not a recalculated
minimum risk: proportional release could understate remaining fixed exit fees.
Cash and carrying basis still release proportionally on partial exits.
Zero/unknown planned risk or stressed risk below planned risk cannot establish
a reservation; reconciliation flags missing position/orphaned-reservation risk
and prevents further entries. Higher fill prices, adverse FX or excess entry
fees cannot use an old authorization. One instrument cannot have another
pending or open entry in this initial policy.

The GBP 1,000 capital is configurable and independent of any broker balance.
Initial planned-risk budgets use that fixed capital denominator:
0.5% per trade (GBP 5), 1% daily threshold (GBP 10), 1% concurrent (GBP 10).
The additional synthetic policy examples cap new allocation at 25%, apply a 2%
notional gap stress and cap stressed risk at 2% of initial capital. These are
provisional software-testing assumptions, not optimal settings or guaranteed
loss limits; no policy is automatically adjusted by AI or research outcomes.

Daily accounting rolls at UTC midnight. Daily P&L is equity change from the
day's opening marked equity. The daily loss policy sums the negative portions
of daily realized P&L and daily unrealized contribution, without offsetting one
with gains in the other. At the threshold it rejects new risk; before entry it
also checks that today's losses plus proposed planned risk fit the threshold.
It cannot prevent a gap in an existing position. Overnight carry is not intended;
unresolved positions and prior-day losses need further policy review before
multi-day real-data research.

The risk engine independently verifies snapshot consistency, known session,
data/FX freshness, asset/quantity/tick rules, no shorts/leverage, affordability,
existing positions/reservations, per-trade/concurrent/daily budgets, stress cap,
duplicate intents and kill-switch state. Risk decisions include quantities,
planned/stressed risk, capital usage, policy/snapshot versions and timestamps.
No approval flag or supplied RiskDecision is accepted by authorize().
Ledger accounting primitives are internal simulation tools, not authorization
or broker execution APIs; arbitrary Python code is not treated as a sandbox.

## Reports and limitations

JSON reports retain dataset/configuration hashes, settings, complete risk
decisions, fill history, audit events, rejection reasons and quality counters.
Configuration fingerprints include the effective calendar manifest, sorted by
venue/date independently of entry order: explicit holiday/closed dates, UTC
opens/closes, timezone names/offsets, half-day flags, calendar schema/version and
the reject-unknown-date policy. A closed date differs from an absent/unknown date.
Injected calendars must expose deterministic JSON-compatible provenance; missing
provenance prevents a report. This describes synthetic assumptions, not validated
exchange schedules. The fill-model version also records quote-stop tie priority
and latched-stop behavior. Reproducing a run still requires matching source code.
They include opening capital, ending equity, gross/net P&L, explicit costs,
entry counts, exit win/loss counts, observed-equity drawdown, cash utilisation
and risk-limit events. Drawdown samples observed marks, not every unknown tick.
Daily threshold events record a breach transition, not every subsequent candle.
If positions remain, ending equity includes their last marks and is not
equivalent to liquidated cash or a completed-trade result.

Missing capabilities: real/point-in-time market data, authoritative calendars,
corporate-action and historical-universe processing, calibrated expected returns,
multiple strategies, production walk-forward statistics, persistence/restart
recovery, settlement, queueing/market impact, live monitoring and any broker
execution. FX trading and ISA compatibility remain separate future work.
